# Phase 2 Originality Audit (literature/lit-theory-draft-lrh.md)

Verdict: 1 high, 2 moderate, 2 low issues. Checked against the 30 anchors; only abstract or snippet text was available.

## High
1. **Colmer (colmer2025nice), para "What we know."** Draft: "conditional on education, workers do not earn more in clean firms than in legacy energy firms, and that moves from legacy to clean firms are rare." The source has a 13-word verbatim run ("conditional on education, workers do not earn more in clean firms than in"). The dangling "and that" also shows it was lifted from the source's syntax. Rewrite: "Linked employer-employee data show that clean-energy firms pay no more than legacy energy firms once education is held constant, and that few workers move from legacy to clean employers [@colmer2025nice]."

## Moderate
2. **Curtis & Marinescu 21%.** Draft: "solar and wind jobs are created in occupations that pay about 21 percent more than the average job." This follows the source's clause ("jobs are created in occupations that are about 21% higher paying than average") almost word for word. There is no 8-word run, but it is patchwork. The source says "green jobs," not "solar and wind" (a faithfulness point for another auditor). Rewrite: "@curtis2023green estimate that green postings cluster in occupations whose average pay is roughly a fifth above the labor-market mean."
3. **Krueger & Summers (krueger1988efficiency).** Draft: "even after accounting for labor quality, working conditions, and firm size." This reuses the source's opening ("even after allowing for ... labor quality, working conditions ... firm size") and its item order. No 8-word run. Rewrite: "Industry wage differentials persist when worker skill, job conditions, and employer size are held constant, which suggests that high-wage industries share rents [@krueger1988efficiency]."

## Low
4. **Curtis et al. <1%.** Draft "Fewer than one percent of workers leaving carbon-intensive jobs move into green ones" mirrors the structure of the source ("less than 1% of all workers who leave a dirty job ... transition to a green job"). It is short, attributed, and has no verbatim run. Optional rewrite: "Green jobs absorb under one percent of those who exit dirty jobs [@curtis2024workers]."
5. **Vona.** "green-intensive employment in U.S. local labor markets" shares a 7-word run with the source ("green employment in US local labor markets"). That is under the 8-word threshold. "Modest" is the draft's own gloss. Consider "across U.S. commuting-zone labor markets" if the source supports it, or keep as is.

## Clear
- **DOE growth sentence:** no anchor holds the source passage, so overlap cannot be tested. Do not treat this as cleared until the USEER growth text is anchored.
- **USEER union figures:** the draft does not copy the table or sentence wording. Only the wind 12% and nuclear 19% anchors exist, so the 16-19, 11-14, and 13 percent ranges are unanchored (a faithfulness gap, not an originality gap).
- **Sicotte, York, Western, Card, BLS:** fully paraphrased, with no shared run of 8 or more words.
