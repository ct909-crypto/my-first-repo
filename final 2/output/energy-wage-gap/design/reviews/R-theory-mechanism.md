# Theory-Mechanism Review (H1-H5)

**Overall:** The design is a sound descriptive pay-gap study, but the pattern of results mostly cannot separate durable-inequality/institutions (DI) from ecological modernization (EM) or from compositional/selection accounts. Union coverage, the core mechanism, is never measured in the models.

## CRITICAL

1. **Mechanism never observed (H1-H3).** The DI account rests on bargaining, internal labor markets and union coverage. Coverage appears only as aggregate DOE figures in the theory text. Cells have no union, tenure, establishment-size or region measures. A negative within-occupation gap is equally what you would expect from selection (younger, newer, less-tenured, lower-wage-region workforces at solar/wind plants) or from regional wage levels. Fix: add a cell-level or technology-level union/ILM proxy (DOE coverage by technology, BLS union tables by industry). Test a dose-response pattern, in which the gap tracks coverage across technologies. Reframe H1-H3 as "consistent with" DI and rank the selection alternatives explicitly.

2. **H4 cannot discriminate DI from EM.** "No narrowing" is a null against an MDES of about 0.06 log points, so a failure to reject cannot support DI. The decision rule retains H4 by default, which means low power favors the DI account. Fix: an equivalence test (TOST) with a substantive margin. State what narrowing magnitude EM predicts over 2021-24, and whether subsidy-linked prevailing-wage effects should appear only in post-IRA builds. Use the subsidy-tied technologies (solar, wind) against hydro as a difference-in-differences contrast.

## MAJOR

3. **H2 pattern is not diagnostic.** "Solar and wind negative, hydro about 0" is also predicted by age-of-plant, firm-size, and small-cell composition explanations, and by EM's "new industry not yet upgraded." The hydro null (CI within +/-0.05) is another underpowered equivalence claim. Fix: make hydro a formal equivalence test. Add a prediction that separates DI from the selection account (nuclear/fossil premium vs. their union coverage).

4. **H3 lacks a competing prediction.** Larger blue-collar gaps also follow from product-market competition and construction-contractor labor markets (project-based solar EPC employment), not only from union compression. Class is a coarse proxy for bargaining. Fix: use the SOC-major gradient with the union-density gradient, and check whether the gap vanishes for occupations with no union premium.

5. **H5 is ill-defined.** The composition component of the Kitagawa decomposition is not the same as "occupational composition conceals the gap." The hypothesis also rests on Curtis's occupational-mix claim, not on a model. Fix: state the sign of the composition term (positive) and its share as the prediction. Drop or downgrade the "at least as large" wording unless a test on the difference M1 vs. M3 is pre-specified. Off-support employment, which can be large in a thin-cell industry, needs a bound.

6. **Cell aggregation and ecological fallacy.** National establishment-pooled cells cannot distinguish within-establishment from between-establishment pay, so the relational mechanism (employer effects) is inferred, not tested. Fix: limit claims to industry-by-occupation pay and say so in the theory.

## MINOR

7. H1's renewable definition lumps hydro with solar and wind while H2 says hydro should differ; H1 is diluted by construction. Make technology the primary contrast, or pre-specify the solar/wind-vs-fossil contrast.
8. Missing implications: dispersion (P90-P10 compression under unions, a reported alt outcome but tied to no hypothesis), employment-growth gradient by occupation, occupation-level entry of new cells, and nuclear vs. fossil union-coverage ordering.
9. Untestable with cell data: worker sorting, tenure, bargaining power, and establishment wage-setting. State these as scope conditions.
