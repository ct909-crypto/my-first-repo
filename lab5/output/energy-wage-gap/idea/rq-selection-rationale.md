# RQ Selection Rationale

Four candidates were evaluated by five independent reviewer lenses (theorist, methodologist, domain expert, ERSS editor, devil's advocate). No candidate received a fatal flaw. Every candidate was rated AT RISK by the devil's advocate for reasons that design choices can mitigate.

**Why RQ-A.** It answers the user's core question (renewable vs nonrenewable wage gaps net of occupation, and their 2021-2025 trend) and has the strongest ERSS fit (editor: Strong fit, Strong appeal). Refining it absorbed the reviewers' top-ranked components: RQ-C heterogeneity became the analytic core (domain expert and editor ranked it first), and the RQ-B decomposition became a bridging analysis (theorist and methodologist ranked it first, but the editor judged it too methodological to stand alone).

**Cross-agent agreement applied.**
1. Detailed SOC cells only. The proposal's 797-cell 2025 model mixed SOC aggregation levels and double-counted workers.
2. OEWS May estimates pool six semiannual panels over three years. May 2021 (Nov 2018-May 2021 panels) and May 2024 (Nov 2021-May 2024 panels) share no panels, so their contrast is the primary trend test; all five years enter secondary models with period interactions.
3. Standard errors clustered by detailed occupation; wages deflated to 2025 dollars with CPI-U.
4. Geothermal and biomass (3-14 cells each) are pooled for period-specific subtype estimates, and no convergence claims are made for them.
5. The distributional question (percentiles) is demoted to robustness because of ecological-inference risk.
6. Claims are descriptive. Institutional mechanisms are discussed as interpretations consistent with the patterns, not tested effects, and nothing is framed as an "IRA effect."

**Rejected or demoted.** RQ-B stands alone poorly (fit 6/10). RQ-C is merged into RQ-A. RQ-D (fit 3/10) becomes robustness only.

**Literature coverage caveat.** No local reference library is configured (Tier 1 unavailable), and no full-text index exists (Tier 1b not run). The novelty assessment ("GAP") rests on OpenAlex/CrossRef and web searches. Closest competitors: Curtis & Marinescu (2023), which uses occupation-average wages; Colmer, Lyubich & Voorheis (2025), using LEHD earnings; Vona, Marin & Consoli (2019), on green-task premiums; and Lehmann (2020), which reports sector medians without occupation adjustment.
