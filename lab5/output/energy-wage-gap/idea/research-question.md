# Research Question

**Selected question (RQ-A, refined).** Among U.S. electric power generation workers in 2021-2025, how large is the gap in occupation-specific mean annual wage between the renewable versus nonrenewable generation sector when detailed occupation is held constant, how does that gap differ across generation technologies and occupational classes, and did it narrow, widen, or persist as renewable employment roughly doubled?

- **X:** renewable (hydro, solar, wind, geothermal, biomass) versus nonrenewable (fossil fuel, nuclear) generation sector; secondarily, the specific technology.
- **Y:** occupation-specific mean annual wage (log, CPI-U deflated) in industry-by-detailed-occupation cells.
- **Expected direction:** negative renewable gap within occupation, largest for blue-collar occupations and newer technologies, near zero for hydro, persistent over time.
- **Mechanism (interpretive):** institutional pay-setting. Legacy utility-owned, unionized, capital-intensive plants share rents; newer merchant or project-financed wind and solar operations pay less. The rival account, ecological modernization with subsidy-driven labor demand, predicts convergence or a premium.
- **Scope:** national OEWS estimates for NAICS 221111-221118, May 2021-May 2025; the unit is the industry x detailed SOC x year cell.
- **Claim strength:** descriptive. Worker selection and establishment traits are unobserved.
- **Target journal:** *Energy Research & Social Science* (imported custom profile).

Components: (1) headline within-occupation gap and its persistence; (2) bridging decomposition of the raw gap into occupational composition and within-occupation pay; (3) heterogeneity by technology and occupational class; (4) robustness, including percentiles, precision weights, balanced occupations, and inclusion of the "other" sector.
