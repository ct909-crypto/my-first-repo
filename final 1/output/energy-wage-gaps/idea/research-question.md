# Research Question

**Selected RQ (RQ1).** Within U.S. electric power generation (NAICS 2211), how large are the hourly wage gaps between renewable generation industries (solar, wind, hydroelectric, geothermal, biomass) and non-renewable generation industries (fossil fuel and nuclear), do these gaps persist within detailed occupations, how do they differ across individual energy sources and occupational groups, and how did they change between 2021 and 2025?

- **X:** renewable versus non-renewable generation industry (and individual six-digit generation source)
- **Y:** real mean hourly wage (log, 2025 dollars) of occupation-industry cells
- **Expected relation:** Competing expectations: a green-premium account predicts renewable > non-renewable within occupation; a legacy rent-sharing account predicts renewable < non-renewable, with nuclear highest and hydro closest to fossil; the gap is expected to narrow over 2021-2025 if renewable segments mature.
- **Mechanism:** Legacy fossil and nuclear generation is a mature, capital-intensive, heavily regulated and comparatively unionized segment with large incumbent firms and rent-sharing, whereas the newer solar and wind segments are younger, more contractor- and developer-based, less unionized and have flatter internal labor markets; establishment wage-setting rather than occupational skill should therefore produce within-occupation wage gaps that may narrow as renewable segments mature and as IRA prevailing-wage provisions take effect.
- **Scope:** national OEWS estimates, May 2021-May 2025; unit = detailed occupation x six-digit generation industry x year.
- **Claim strength:** descriptive (establishment-side wage gaps, not causal effects of working in renewables).
- **Target journal:** Energy Policy (full-length article, <= 8,000 words; target 10-12 single-spaced pages).
- **Data:** BLS OEWS national industry-specific estimates (nat5d_6d files) for NAICS 221111-221118.

Note on sector coverage: the OEWS files separate electric power generation into eight six-digit industries. Petroleum-fired generation is not a separate industry and is contained within Fossil Fuel Electric Power Generation (221112); transmission and distribution (22112) are not published at the six-digit level in these files and are outside scope.
